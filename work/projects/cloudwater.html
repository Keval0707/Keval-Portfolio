01 / Project overview

Not just a store. A connected care journey.

Cloudwater’s exported screens show a product built around the entire life of a water purifier: shopping or renting, setting up a device, finding technicians, scheduling service, and keeping AMC coverage visible over time.

Instead of presenting unrelated features, this case study follows how those destinations could work together as one coherent mobile experience.

The design opportunity
How might customers find, maintain and protect a purifier without switching between disconnected journeys?

This question is an analytical framing drawn from the visible interface. No original research brief or interviews were included in the upload.

04 / Designing the entry

Introduce value before asking for details.

Three onboarding screens communicate the app’s main promises: simpler service, AMC tracking and genuine purifier shopping. Account entry follows with sign-up, OTP verification and device-aware profile setup.

  • 1Focused onboarding: one message per panel, paired with the water-themed illustration system.
  • 2OTP verification: a recognizable code-entry bottom sheet creates a distinct authentication state.
  • 3Optional device context: purifier brand, model and serial details appear in profile setup.
05 / Product decision-making

One catalogue. Two different commitments.

The store provides explicit Buy and Rent modes. Product detail screens expand on features, price or rental terms; the cart and order-review states help surface costs before confirmation.

  • 1Clear mode switching: the store distinguishes buying from renting rather than mixing transaction models.
  • 2Information layering: product cards are compact; detail screens reveal conditions and benefits progressively.
  • 3Payment clarity: order summaries display product, address, charges and available payment choices.
06 / Rental checkout deep dive

Five steps, with one clear destination.

The rental journey is the most explicit multi-step transaction in the exports. A progress tracker moves through Identity → Address → Agreement → Review → Payment. Device and pricing summaries support checking the commitment before enabling AutoPay.

  • 1Identity: front and back Aadhaar upload fields and a consent checkbox.
  • 2Address: saved addresses can be selected or another address added.
  • 3Agreement + review: plan details, terms and a breakdown are visible before payment.
  • 4AutoPay: a confirmation screen communicates successful activation.
07 / Service and support

Turn a maintenance issue into a trackable visit.

The service screens cover installation, repair and filter replacement. Users can select a brand/model, provide serial details and issue photos, pick a date, and later inspect booking status and technician information.

  • 1Device context: brand, model and images support more specific requests.
  • 2Scheduling: a day-based interface makes the visit date a visible decision.
  • 3Follow-through: bookings, service history and ratings extend the journey after the visit.
08 / Product aftercare

Protection is an ongoing relationship.

AMC experiences show active and expiring device coverage, plan comparison and renewal options. Separate device views cover rented and purchased purifiers, so service history and protection remain associated with a specific product.

  • 1Contextual status: AMC cards expose coverage, dates and renewal actions.
  • 2Tier comparison: Basic Labour, Semi-Comprehensive and Fully Comprehensive appear as explicit plan choices.
  • 3Device continuity: active rentals and owned purifiers have dedicated management screens.
09 / State design

Support the quiet moments, not only the happy path.

The exports also document empty states, confirmation states, filters, cancellations and feedback. Those moments shape how trustworthy a service app feels during real use.

12 / Interface decisions

Design patterns that connect the product.

Across the exported screens, three patterns recur: progressive disclosure for complex choices, context-based device management, and deliberate system states.

↗

Progressive disclosure

Shopping cards lead to richer product details; rental and service tasks reveal one set of requirements at a time.

◈

Device-aware experiences

Purifier, brand, model, warranty and plan information reappear in contexts where they matter most.

◎

Designed system feedback

Empty states, filter sheets, success views, cancellation prompts and ratings support a range of outcomes.

13 / Product logic

The interface has to work at different moments of commitment.

Buying a purifier, beginning a rental and requesting a technician involve different kinds of uncertainty. The exported screens show different approaches for each moment.

01 · Before committing

Explain the offer

Buy and Rent are separate catalogue modes. Product pages disclose features, prices or rental plans so users can compare without switching products.

  • Decision: buy versus rent
  • Reference: Shop_buy, Shop_rent, product detail screens
  • Potential check: can visitors articulate the commitment?
02 · During commitment

Keep progress visible

Rental onboarding exposes identity verification, address, agreement, order review and payment as separate stages. This supports an otherwise high-friction task.

  • Decision: divide the process into named steps
  • Reference: Step_1 through Step_5 screens
  • Potential check: fees, authorization and dropout points
03 · After commitment

Make aftercare tangible

The app continues into booking details, AMC coverage, device lists, service history and order tracking. The design therefore goes beyond one-time checkout.

  • Decision: persistent service and device records
  • Reference: Booking detail, Active AMC, All device
  • Potential check: status comprehension and next steps
14 / High-stakes flow

Make a five-step rental process understandable.

Clear stages reduce uncertainty—not necessarily effort.

The exported rental checkout contains five labeled stages, starting with identity verification and ending with AutoPay setup. A stepper establishes where someone is in the process, while agreement and review screens reveal the terms before authorization.

  • What works: named stages, a review screen, a distinct payment authorization step.
  • Potential friction: identity documentation and recurring payment terms may require more explanation.
  • Opportunity: show a transparent cost recap and explain AutoPay timing near the final action.

16 / Experience quality

Design for the edge cases, too.

Success screens, empty states and cancellations are present in the export set. They deserve the same clarity and care as the polished discovery flows. These checks are recommended work, not completed QA findings.

01 / EMPTY STATESGive people a next action.

Empty cart, empty search and empty dashboard screens should make recovery obvious without making users feel stuck.

02 / DESTRUCTIVE ACTIONSConfirm consequential choices.

Cancellation confirmation can clarify what changes, whether money is affected and how to undo a mistake when possible.

03 / LONG SCROLLMaintain readable details.

Several exports are long pages. Sticky actions, section headings and accessible reading order should be checked on real devices.

04 / ACCESSIBILITYMake controls easier to use.

Review contrast, error messages, keyboard and screen reader labels, and comfortable touch targets for 393px mobile screens.

14 / Critical reflection

A comprehensive concept. The next step is validation.

The screens demonstrate broad product coverage and a coherent interface language. They do not, on their own, prove task success, user satisfaction, business lift or a shipped implementation.

What the design shows well

  • A full lifecycle—from onboarding and product discovery to service history and AMC.
  • Clear Buy/Rent modes and a structured five-step rental setup.
  • Device-specific detail across protection, service and ownership.
  • Dedicated empty, loading-adjacent, success and cancellation states.

What I would validate next

  • Whether first-time users understand the difference between Buy, Rent, Service and AMC.
  • Drop-off and comprehension around Aadhaar verification, agreement and AutoPay.
  • Service booking completion, date selection and failure recovery.
  • Readable long-form details, accessible tap targets and clarity on small screens.
